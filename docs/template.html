What communities actually signed.

Model agreements say what a town should ask a data center for. This is what signed deals delivered, clause by clause, with a receipt for every term.

Why the name: Mark Cuban has said a lot of data centers will end up as pickleball courts if efficiency gains keep coming. The terms that matter are the ones written for that day.

Scored by Aaron Peabody, who read the documents. I run technology for a paving and site-work contractor, so data centers are a customer for work like mine; the terms I rank highest, teardown bonds and minimum bills, do nothing for my company. The bar is my opinion and it lives in a file anyone can argue with. Corrections go in CORRECTIONS.md and there are already ten.

The bar what each term has to include to count as met

These bars are a position, not a law. They live in a plain text file in the repository, and anyone can propose a change by pull request.

Limits read this before you quote it

  1. Small sample. A handful of deals is a start, not a survey. Do not read a pattern here as the national picture until the count is much higher.
  2. One reader per document. "Read from the signed document" means a person read it and cited sections. It does not mean two people agreed. Corrections are welcome and expected.
  3. Deals change. Agreements get amended, voided, and superseded. Each record carries the date it was last reviewed. Check the primary document before you act on a term.
  4. The bar is an opinion. A deal that falls short here may still be a good deal for that town. The stamps say whether a term is present, not whether the whole deal was wise.
  5. Unknown is not a failure. A question mark means nobody has found the answer yet. Many terms live in tariffs, side letters, or records requests, not the headline agreement.
