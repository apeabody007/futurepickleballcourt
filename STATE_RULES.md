# State rules that set a floor

Some protections come from the state or the utility tariff, not the local deal. When a deal file says a term is governed by one of these, this is the reference. Dates and bill numbers were checked against the sources linked; where a fact could not be confirmed it says so.

## Florida, SB 484 (Chapter 2026-65)

Signed May 7, 2026, effective July 1, 2026. Defines a large load customer as 50 MW or more of anticipated monthly peak at a single location, colocation counted together. Utility tariffs must reasonably ensure each such customer bears its own full cost of service, including connection, incremental transmission and generation, and operations, with nonpayment risk not borne by other ratepayers. The Public Service Commission may approve contributions in aid of construction, minimum demand charges, financial guarantees, take-or-pay provisions, and minimum contract terms with early-termination fees. Utilities must file compliant tariffs by October 1, 2026. Consumptive-use permits for 50 MW-plus data centers require reclaimed water when feasible and a hearing before approval.

What it does not do: ban nondisclosure agreements. The filed bill barred state agencies from NDAs on data center projects. The enacted text drops that and instead removes the optional second 12-month confidentiality extension for data center projects, leaving the base 12 months intact.

Sources: [enrolled text](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026/484/BillText/er/PDF), [Senate summary](https://www.flsenate.gov/Committees/BillSummaries/2026/html/484), [Holland and Knight on the filed version](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/proposed-florida-legislation-creates-new-regulatory-framework), [The Invading Sea on the NDA change](https://www.theinvadingsea.com/2026/05/08/data-centers-florida-legislature-sb-484-desantis-utilities-energy-costs-nondisclosure-agreements/). First test: Duke Energy Florida's large-load tariff, which the Public Counsel and one commissioner called noncompliant at an August 2026 hearing ([WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2026-08-26/new-state-law-protect-consumers-data-center-costs-faces-first-test)).

## Texas, SB 6 (2025)

Signed June 20, 2025. Directs the Public Utility Commission of Texas to set interconnection standards for loads of 75 MW or more, requires a transmission screening study fee of at least $100,000, requires financial commitments for transmission infrastructure and a contribution to interconnection costs, and lets ERCOT curtail large loads with backup generation in emergencies. The proposed implementing rule (16 TAC 25.194, published March 2026) would require $50,000 per MW in security to enter a study and 100 percent contribution in aid of construction for direct interconnection costs. Final adoption of that rule was not confirmed as of September 2026.

Sources: [bill history](https://capitol.texas.gov/BillLookup/History.aspx?LegSess=89R&Bill=SB6), [McGuireWoods](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2025/7/texas-senate-bill-6-significantly-expands-regulatory-oversight-over-large-loads-in-ercot/), [Greenberg Traurig on the proposed rule](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/3/texas-senate-bill-6-update-what-data-centers-large-load-customers-should-know-about-proposed-interconnection-standards).

## Ohio, AEP Ohio data center tariff (PUCO Case 24-508-EL-ATA)

Approved July 9, 2025. Applies to new data center loads above 25 MW. Minimum billing of up to 85 percent of contracted capacity regardless of usage, a ramp period of up to four years, a contract term of at least eight years after ramp, an exit fee of three years of minimum charges, and enhanced collateral for customers below A-minus credit. This is the clearest example of a minimum bill that survives a tenant walking, and it came from the regulator, not a local deal.

Sources: [PUCO](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/OHPUC/bulletins/3e8bb79), [Vorys](https://www.vorys.com/publication-public-utilities-commission-of-ohio-authorizes-tariff-for-aep-ohios-data-center-customers-requires-end-of-moratorium-on-new-services-for-data-centers), [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/regulator-approves-aep-ohios-landmark-data-center-tariff/).

## New Jersey, Data Center Fair Share (P.L. 2026, c.32)

Signed July 7, 2026. Creates a separate ratepayer class for data centers of 50 MW or more, new and existing, and directs the Board of Public Utilities to set tariff standards so they pay for their own energy use and grid infrastructure. Reported terms include financial guarantees covering 85 percent of requested service for ten years and curtailment ahead of residential customers in emergencies. A companion law signed August 27, 2026 requires semiannual water and energy reports. Prevailing wage on data center work was enacted January 20, 2026.

Sources: [Governor's release](https://www.nj.gov/governor/news/2026/20260707a.shtml), [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/new-jersey-lawmakers-send-data-center-tariff-bill-to-governor/824191/), [NJBIZ on reporting](https://njbiz.com/nj-data-center-energy-water-reporting-law/), [Local Finance Notice 2026-13](https://www.nj.gov/dca/dlgs/lfns/2026/2026-13.pdf).

## Virginia (2026 session)

HB 153 and SB 94 require, before a rezoning or special use permit for a facility of 100 MW or more, an assessment of its sound profile on homes and schools within 500 feet, and let localities require water, agricultural, and historic-resource assessments. HB 1393 and SB 253 originally directed Dominion to assign new generation costs to customers with 25 MW or more of demand; the governor's amendments, accepted April 22, 2026, replaced the explicit cost shift with State Corporation Commission authority to decide whether high-load customers pay their fair share. The budget kept the sales and use tax exemption and added an electricity consumption tax on data center operators of $0.011 per kWh through June 30, 2028, capped at $600 million a year. Chapter numbers for the cost-allocation bills were not confirmed.

Sources: [MultiState](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/3/30/virginia-lawmakers-pass-15-data-center-bills-as-tax-exemption-fight-looms), [Virginia Mercury on the amendments](https://virginiamercury.com/2026/04/23/va-lawmakers-ok-governors-tweaks-to-major-energy-bills-reject-health-and-labor-bill-amendments/), [Holland and Knight on the budget](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/virginia-preserves-data-center-tax-incentive).

## Michigan, Public Act 207 of 2024

Effective April 17, 2025. The enterprise data center sales and use tax exemption is conditioned on at least $250 million of investment, at least 30 jobs at 150 percent of the regional median wage maintained through 2050, no local property tax benefits without every affected local body's approval, a green building certification within three years, municipal water from a system with capacity, clean energy procurement equal to 90 percent of forecast use that passes no costs to other customers, annual reporting, and revocation with repayment of all exemptions plus interest. This is the most complete set of conditions attached to a state incentive we found.

Sources: [enrolled act](https://legislature.mi.gov/documents/2023-2024/publicact/pdf/2024-PA-0207.pdf), [Planet Detroit on the criticisms](https://planetdetroit.org/2024/12/michigan-senate-passes-tax-breaks/).
