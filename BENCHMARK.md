## What the deals show

**Nobody posts a teardown bond.** Not one of the 22 agreements requires a bond, escrow, or letter of credit to pay for demolition and site restoration if the operator walks away. If a campus goes dark, the land is the community's problem.

**Community money, decided without the community.** 8 of the 22 deals set up money for the community. In none of them does a resident or community organization hold a seat on the body that decides how it is spent.

**The one real protection was not negotiated locally.** 7 of the 22 deals carry a minimum electric bill, the term that keeps a tenant paying if it stops drawing power (City of New Albany, City of Papillion and Sarpy County, City of Port Washington, Pima County, Richland Parish, Village of Los Lunas, Village of Mount Pleasant). Every one of them comes from the utility's own rate structure, not from anything the city or county negotiated, and how far each survives an actual departure varies.

**Secrecy is normal.** 8 of the 22 communities signed a nondisclosure agreement. Some barred officials from saying that talks were happening at all, and one required the city to destroy its own notes.

**Present is not the same as binding.** 52 terms across these agreements exist but are softened by language like good faith, commercially reasonable, or sole discretion. A term with an escape hatch reads as a protection and functions as a preference.

**Not scored: Hire your own lawyers and technical advisors.** This is a decision a town makes before it signs, not a term inside the agreement, so there is nothing in these documents to score. It may be the highest-value item on his list.

**Not scored: Assess the entire planned build-out, not just phase one.** Also a process question. It does show up indirectly: several agreements here write payment schedules for the first buildings only and go quiet about the rest.

**Not scored: Demand guarantees from a financially credible parent company.** No column tracks this yet because too few agreements address it to compare. Where it exists it is noted inside the deal, as in Richland Parish, where advocates argue the guarantee stopped reaching an entity with assets after the ownership changed.


## The strongest terms anyone has actually signed

The strongest version of each term that someone has actually signed, or the closest thing to it. These are judgment calls about real documents, not invented language, and they change by pull request like everything else here.

**Community fund** (City of St. Louis, MO, Contour). $30 per square foot of approved data center, so the fund scales with what actually gets built instead of being a flat headline number. [p. 2, Other Community Benefits (Financial)](https://www.stlouis-mo.gov/government/departments/mayor/documents/upload/CBA-provisions.pdf)

**Clawbacks** (City of St. Louis, MO, Contour). Liquidated damages of $2,000 per job short of a 25, 50, then 100 job ladder for 20 years, plus $15,000 for every $100,000 of tax revenue missed. Money, per unit missed, not an all-or-nothing default. [p. 1, Tax Revenue & Job Requirements](https://www.stlouis-mo.gov/government/departments/mayor/documents/upload/CBA-provisions.pdf)

**Decommissioning** (Pima County, AZ, Beale Infrastructure). No deal on record posts a bond. The closest is the neighboring town of Marana, which conditioned its approval of the same developer on removing all hardware and securing the property if the use stops. [full agreement](https://content.civicplus.com/api/assets/7477affb-e443-412c-b8db-1bc3bb3794f6)

**Grid costs** (City of New Albany, OH, Meta). Billing demand of at least 85 percent of peak for a twelve year term, an exit fee of 36 months of minimum charges, and collateral of half the total minimum charges unless the customer is investment grade. [Schedule DCT, sheets 223-1 to 223-7](https://ohiocapitaljournal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/modified-tariff-data-centers.pdf)

**Water** (City of Lancaster, PA, CoreWeave). A hard cap of 20,000 gallons a day per campus written into the agreement, against a prior industrial user on the same site that drew 100,000 to 125,000. [Section 3.4](https://www.cityoflancasterpa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Lancaster-CBA-Draft.pdf)

**Noise** (Loudoun County, VA, Cross Mill). 55 dBA at the residential lot line, a study before the first site plan and again within six months of every occupancy permit, mitigation within 60 days of an exceedance, all certified by an acoustical engineer. [Proffer II.E, noise; Proffer III.C.b, generators; staff report p. 2 item 6, setback](https://loudoun.granicus.com/MetaViewer.php?view_id=89&clip_id=7984&meta_id=266768)

**Jobs** (Richland Parish, LA, Meta). A job ladder of 100 by 2028 rising to 500 by 2032 at 150 percent of the state average wage, secured by the state's right to raise the purchase price or take the land back. [p. 1, project description](https://lpscpubvalence.lpsc.louisiana.gov/portal/PSC/ViewFile?fileId=nDWn/juc2+A%3D)

**Local contracting** (City of Cedar Rapids, IA, QTS). A $2,000,000 payment and performance bond posted within ten business days, plus $117,099.90 for damage already done and about 2.6 miles of road rebuilt at the developer's sole cost. The county can also draw on the bond for damage it cannot see, at $3.86 per quarry truck trip. [Road use agreement Section 3.A, Board of Supervisors packet October 27, 2025](https://linncoia.portal.civicclerk.com/event/7860/files)

**Transparency** (Loudoun County, VA, Cross Mill). No NDA anywhere in the record, the whole file public, and compliance verified by outside professionals rather than self-certified. [Proffer II.E noise studies; Proffer V.A.g annual metering reports](https://loudoun.granicus.com/MetaViewer.php?view_id=89&clip_id=7984&meta_id=266768)

**Tax incentives** (Loudoun County, VA, Cross Mill). No abatement at all. Full real property tax plus $4.15 per $100 on computer equipment, which funds roughly $1.3 billion of a $2.9 billion budget, banked against a stabilization fund. [pp. 34 to 35, tax rates and computer equipment revenue; p. 363, stabilization fund](https://www.loudoun.gov/DocumentCenter/View/221475/FY-2027-Adopted-Budget-Document)


## What the floor asks for

- **Community fund**: A fund that scales with the project, with a community seat on the body that spends it
- **Clawbacks**: Incentives come back if the facility stops operating, not only if a jobs number is missed
- **Decommissioning**: A bond, escrow, or letter of credit that pays for teardown and site restoration if the operator walks
- **Grid costs**: The operator pays for its own interconnection and carries a minimum bill that survives it leaving
- **Water**: A stated cap or closed-loop cooling, plus public reporting
- **Noise**: A numeric limit measured at the nearest residence or property line
- **Jobs**: Binding job commitments with a consequence, not projections
- **Local contracting**: Local subcontracting goals and haul-route road repair paid by the operator
- **Transparency**: No NDA, the agreement is public, and an independent audit on a schedule
- **Tax incentives**: A but-for test before any abatement, with the forgone revenue stated
